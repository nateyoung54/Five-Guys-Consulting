Section One

Project Background

1.1   Problem / Opportunity Description

BP Burger is experiencing an increase in demand that its current systems can't handle. These systems include:

01
Inventory Management – Inventory is not being accurately tracked, leading to wasted ingredients and the risk of customers being served spoiled or unavailable items.
02
Order Management – Orders are taken by hand on paper and transferred to the kitchen. This process is slow and prone to errors during busy hours.
03
Point of Sales – Transactions are handled manually using a cash register and handwritten tickets, which slows service and increases errors. The system also does not collect detailed sales data.
04
Forecasting – There is a lack of reliable sales and demand data, making it difficult to predict inventory needs and customer trends. As a result, ordering is reactive rather than data-driven, leading to overstocking, stockouts, and inefficient supplier orders.

1.2   Benefits

With a better system in place, BP Burger can:

  • Track sales to see frequent orders
  • Forecast supplies to see what is needed in the future
  • Manage inventory online to ensure all supplies are used efficiently
  • Automate reordering to get supplies on time
  • Implement an online point of sale system to track all orders
  • Ensure correct ingredients, increasing customer satisfaction
  • Track and improve customer satisfaction

1.3   Goals

We want to bring an online system that integrates different business operations to limit mistakes in orders, inventory, and forecasting. The system will limit cost spending and should provide a more efficient and profitable business model.

1.4   Stakeholders and Clients

  • Internal (Active participants): The owner and the server — both must support and be involved with this project.
  • External (Interest): Customers, since their orders would be more accurate.
  • Directly affected: The owner, server, supplier, and customers.
  • Affected if project is not undertaken: Everyone — the owner, server, customers, and supplier.

Section Three

Project Plan

3.1   Approach and Methodology

The project will follow a structured Systems Development Life Cycle (SDLC) approach to ensure an organized and effective transition from BP Burger's paper-based processes to a fully functional electronic system. The strategy is to move sequentially through planning, analysis, design, development, testing, and implementation while maintaining continuous communication with stakeholders (Bud and Pearl) to ensure the system meets their operational needs.

The project will begin with requirements gathering, where interviews and observations will be conducted to fully understand current workflows, pain points, and desired system capabilities. This will be followed by system design, including the creation of Data Flow Diagrams and Entity Relationship Diagrams to map out how data will move through the system and how it will be structured.

Once the design is finalized, the team will move into development, where the database and application system will be built based on the approved specifications. After development, the system will undergo testing and debugging to identify and correct any errors, ensuring accuracy in inventory tracking, order processing, and reporting.

A pilot implementation will be conducted to gradually transition from the paper-based system to the electronic system, minimizing disruption to daily operations. Finally, the project will conclude with full implementation and training, ensuring that Bud and Pearl are comfortable using the new system, supported by clear documentation.

Primary Tools

Database management software (MySQL) · Programming tools for system development · Diagramming tools (Lucidchart) for DFDs and ERDs · Excel for initial data analysis and forecasting · Basic hardware procurement as needed (computer or tablet for order entry and inventory management)

Appendix

Additional Information

A.1   Assumptions

  • Bud and Pearl will be available for meetings and check-ins during the project.
  • BP Burger will share existing sales records and inventory logs when needed.
  • The client will handle purchasing any needed hardware, like a computer or tablet, before implementation.
  • Wi-Fi or internet will be available at the location.
  • Bud and Pearl will set aside time for training and give feedback on how the system is working.
  • There will be no major menu or supplier changes during the project.

A.2   Requirements Gathering Methods

Three stakeholder groups were identified. The table below shows which method(s) were used for each group and why.

Owners / Management

  • Interview ✓ — Direct access to the decision-makers who know current pain points and goals best.
  • Observation ✓ — Lets us see daily operations firsthand and catch issues not raised in interviews.
  • Survey ✗ — Unnecessary with a small, accessible group; interviews provide richer detail.

Kitchen Staff / Servers

  • Observation ✓ — Best way to see real-time order and kitchen workflow and identify bottlenecks.
  • Interview ✓ — Gives staff a chance to describe recurring problems from their own perspective.
  • Survey ✗ — The group is small enough that direct conversation is more effective than a survey.

Customers

  • Survey ✓ — Efficient way to collect broad feedback from many customers at once.
  • Interview ✗ — Impractical to conduct in-depth interviews during active service hours.
  • Observation ✗ — Watching customers order does not reveal preferences or satisfaction levels.

A.3   Interview Questions

Owners/Management

  • What information do you currently need at the end of the day, and what are the issues with using the paper-based system for this?
  • What features do you want in the new system to improve inventory tracking and ordering?

Customers

  • What aspects of the ordering process are the most important to you when visiting the restaurant?
  • Have you experienced issues with your orders or long wait times, and what could be improved to fix this?

A.4   Suggestions & Implementation Guidance

Training: Do not rush the training when it comes to learning the new system.

Staff Input: Listen to the employees' thoughts on the new system, they will be the ones also using it.

Transition: Try running both the old and new system at the same time for a short period before fully switching over so you can catch any issues early.
